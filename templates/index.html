📐2. Mathematical Formulations & Theoretical Rigor

PageRank & Random Walk with Restart (PPR)
p^{(t+1)} = d \cdot M \cdot p^{(t)} + (1-d) \cdot v

Where $d=0.85$ is the damping factor, $M$ is the column-stochastic transition matrix, and $v$ is the teleportation vector (uniform for global PageRank, or seeded for Personalized PageRank).

Okapi BM25 Probabilistic Relevance
Score(D, Q) = \sum_{q \in Q} IDF(q) \cdot \frac{f(q, D) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{f(q, D) + k_1 \cdot (1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}})}

Penalizes excessively long documents and models sub-linear term frequency saturation using parameters $k_1 = 1.5$ and $b = 0.75$.

Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR)
\text{MMR} = \arg\max_{d \in R \setminus S} \left[ \lambda \cdot \text{Sim}_1(d, Q) - (1-\lambda) \max_{d_j \in S} \text{Sim}_2(d, d_j) \right]

Maximizes recommendation novelty and diversity by penalizing items similar to previously chosen papers in set $S$ ($\lambda=0.7$).

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)
\text{RRF\_Score}(d) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}

Parameter-free rank ensemble across semantic, topological, and user preference rankers with smoothing factor $k=60$.

💬3. Anticipated Viva Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why does SRPS outperform standard keyword search or raw TF-IDF? ▼

Keyword search and TF-IDF suffer from term saturation and lack structural graph context. A paper can be semantically relevant but poorly cited or obsolete. SRPS uses an ensemble that combines Okapi BM25 for text relevance, Personalized PageRank and Kleinberg's HITS for citation authority, temporal recency decay to balance foundational vs new papers, and MMR to prevent redundant results.

Q2: How does Personalized PageRank differ from Global PageRank? ▼

Global PageRank assumes a uniform teleportation probability vector $v = [1/N, \dots, 1/N]^T$, finding universally authoritative papers across the entire corpus. In contrast, Personalized PageRank (Random Walk with Restart) biases teleportation probability toward a specific subset of seed nodes (e.g. papers the active user has authored, bookmarked, or queried), delivering user-personalized authority scores.

Q3: How do you handle newly published papers that have 0 citations (Cold-Start Problem)? ▼

Pure graph methods fail on new papers because they have zero in-degree citations. In SRPS, content-based BM25 and TF-IDF compute high similarity scores regardless of citations. Furthermore, our temporal recency decay function $\exp(-\lambda \Delta t)$ gives a recency bonus, and our bibliometrics engine computes bibliographic coupling based on shared outgoing references, successfully embedding newly published papers into the citation network.

Q4: Why does Power Iteration guarantee convergence in your PageRank implementation? ▼

By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, if a transition matrix is irreducible and aperiodic (primitive), it has a unique stationary distribution with eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = 1$. The damping factor $d=0.85$ transforms any arbitrary directed citation graph into an irreducible, strongly connected graph. Thus, repeated vector-matrix multiplication $p^{(t+1)} = d M p^{(t)} + (1-d)v$ is guaranteed to converge geometrically.

Q5: How did you verify the correctness and reliability of the system? ▼

We implemented an automated test suite with 51 passing unit and integration tests covering graph algorithms (PageRank, HITS, shortest paths), BM25 term weighting, user interest cosine matching, bibliographic coupling, citation velocity, REST API endpoints, and TREC evaluation metrics (NDCG, MAP, MRR).
